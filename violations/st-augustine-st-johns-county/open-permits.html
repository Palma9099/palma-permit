How Palma resolves open permits in St. Augustine

Work inside the City of St. Augustine is permitted by the city's Planning and Building Department, while unincorporated St. Johns County permits online through the Accela Citizen Access portal, so the first step is confirming which authority holds your record. Both adopt the Florida Building Code, and under Florida Statute 553.791 a licensed private provider can perform plan review and inspections. The nation's oldest city has a large historic district, so older-structure review is a routine part of the process here.

St. Augustine is an Atlantic-coast market on the Matanzas River and Intracoastal, so oceanfront and riverfront parcels sit in FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas and the wind-borne debris region. FEMA recently redrew St. Johns County's flood maps for the first time in 14 years, and the county's CRS Class 5 rating earns flood-insurance discounts, so base-flood elevation and the substantial-improvement rule frequently shape a project. Storm and flood work is where open permits, expired permits, and unpermitted repairs surface, and where Palma's coordination saves the most time.
