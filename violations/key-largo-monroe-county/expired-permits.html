How Palma resolves expired permits in Key Largo

Key Largo is unincorporated Upper Keys, so its Authority Having Jurisdiction is the Monroe County Building Department. Monroe County adopts the Florida Building Code through Chapter 6 of its Code of Ordinances and layers on a Land Development Code that governs setbacks, use, and the county's Rate of Growth Ordinance (ROGO/BPAS) - the system that allocates a limited number of new residential building permits each year. Under Florida Statute 553.791 you can also use a licensed private provider to perform plan review and inspections in place of county staff, which often shortens timelines on a stalled project; Monroe publishes the required private-provider notice and inspection forms alongside its permitting fee schedule.

Because nearly all of the Keys sits in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area, Monroe enforces strict floodplain rules: base-flood-elevation requirements and a cumulative 50%-of-value 'substantial improvement' limit that can turn a straightforward renovation into a full-elevation project. The Keys also carry some of the highest design wind speeds in Florida, so opening protection and Florida/Miami-Dade product approvals are central to plan review. These are precisely the local rules behind most unpermitted work, expired permits, and after-the-fact permitting here - and where Palma's coordination saves owners the most time.
